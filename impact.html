From Research to Real-World Use

My work has been used beyond academic publication in public health guidance, clinical resources, professional education, patient advocacy and mainstream media. These examples show how research findings have been translated for different audiences and used to support practical decisions across healthcare, sport and public-facing settings.

The Paradoxical Effect of Creatine Monohydrate on Muscle Damage Markers: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Sports Medicine, 2022  ·  Co-author

  • Washington PostMedia & Public Communication

    Used findings from the meta-analysis to inform public-facing coverage of creatine, exercise recovery and muscle damage, translating technical evidence for a mainstream audience.

  • Operation Supplement SafetyPublic Health Guidance

    Incorporated findings from the review into public guidance for athletes and Service Members on creatine supplementation, recovery and exercise-induced muscle damage.

  • Transparent LabsCommercial & Consumer Content

    Used the review's findings on acute versus longer-term responses to creatine supplementation in consumer-facing content discussing muscle damage, inflammation and recovery.

Effects of Plyometric Jump Training on Balance Performance in Healthy Participants: A Systematic Review With Meta-Analysis

Frontiers in Physiology, 2021  ·  First author

  • Yoga Medicine – "Balance Myths Busted"Practitioner Education

    Included the paper within the evidence base for a practitioner-facing discussion on balance as a trainable physical quality and the role of plyometric training in improving static and dynamic balance.

Changes in Lower Limb Biomechanics Across Various Stages of Maturation and Implications for ACL Injury Risk in Female Athletes: A Systematic Review

Sports Medicine, 2024  ·  First and corresponding author

  • UK Strength and Conditioning AssociationProfessional Guidance

    Used the review to support practitioner guidance on assessing adolescent female athletes, including the importance of biological maturity and neuromuscular development when interpreting ACL-related movement risk.

Influence of Neuromuscular Training Interventions on Jump-Landing Biomechanics and Implications for ACL Injuries in Youth Females: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Sports Medicine, 2025  ·  First author

  • PEDro / Physiotherapy ChoicesClinical & Patient Resource

    Summarised the review for physiotherapy and clinical audiences, highlighting changes in landing mechanics associated with neuromuscular training and making the findings more accessible for clinical interpretation and decision-making.

Post-polio Hospital Admissions in Australia Over a 10-Year Period: An Observational Study and Analysis of Trends by Month, Location, and Comparable Conditions

Journal of Public Health, 2025  ·  Co-author

  • Polio Australia / Polio Health Research DatabasePatient & Advocacy Resource

  • Polio Australia Annual ReportPublic Health & Advocacy Reporting

    Included the study within Polio Australia's reporting of research activity, helping communicate emerging evidence on post-polio health and service use to broader stakeholder audiences.

  • Polio Place / Post-Polio Health InternationalPatient & Advocacy Resource

    Included the paper in its public medical-articles database, making the findings accessible to post-polio communities, advocacy organisations and healthcare professionals.

Effects of Muscle Strengthening and Cardiovascular Fitness Activities for Poliomyelitis Survivors: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2021  ·  First author

  • Academy of Neurologic Physical Therapy – Post-Polio Syndrome Fact SheetClinical Guidance

    Incorporated the review into guidance on strengthening and aerobic exercise for people with post-polio syndrome, supporting individually prescribed and monitored rehabilitation approaches.

  • Academy of Neurologic Physical Therapy – Exercise and Post-Polio Fact SheetPatient Education

    Used the review in a patient-facing resource on exercise after polio, helping communicate evidence around adapting training to symptoms, fatigue and functional capacity.

  • Polio Australia / Post Polio Victoria Clinical GuideClinical Guidance

    Cited the review within exercise guidance for clinicians, supporting carefully prescribed and monitored strengthening programmes for people living with the late effects of polio.

  • Polio Australia Research DatabasePatient & Advocacy Resource

    Featured the review across research categories including cardiovascular health, exercise, late effects of polio and muscle strength, improving access to the evidence for patient and professional audiences.

  • PEDroClinical Evidence Resource

    Indexed the review within a public physiotherapy evidence database, making the findings more discoverable to clinicians and rehabilitation professionals.
